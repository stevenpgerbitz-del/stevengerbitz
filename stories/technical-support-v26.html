02 / Technical Support / The story

I like a good puzzle.

The computer says one thing. The user describes another. Somewhere in the middle is usually the real problem.

A little background

Finding out what actually happened.

I've supported technology in very different environments—from offices at mining operations to classrooms full of devices that all need to be ready before the school day starts. The equipment changes, but the first question is usually the same: what's really going on here?

Sometimes it's a hardware failure. Sometimes it's a configuration, a missing connection, or something that worked yesterday and decided to take today off. I like working through the possibilities instead of guessing and hoping.

And yes, I've asked the occasional “Have you tried turning it off and on again?” Sometimes that really is the answer. Sometimes it's just the beginning.

At Peabody Energy I was part of the onsite IT team supporting three mining operations, working on workstations, printers, phone replacements, and day-to-day support. At Des Moines Public Schools, that problem-solving continues with staff laptops, student iPads, classroom technology, and school device rollouts.

“Sometimes the fix is complicated. Sometimes it really is the cable.”

What it means to me

Technical support is where curiosity becomes useful. I like understanding why a fix works, not just knowing which button made the error disappear.

There's always something new to learn, and there's satisfaction in finding a practical answer that helps someone get on with their day. It isn't about being the smartest person in the room. It's about being willing to keep looking until things make sense.

The work behind the words

Windows troubleshooting, hardware repairs, device preparation, Microsoft Intune, Mosyle MDM, Active Directory support, diagnostics, and clear documentation.
