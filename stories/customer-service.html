03 / Customer Service / The story

There's a person on the other side.

When someone calls frustrated, they're usually frustrated with the situation—not the person trying to help.

A little background

Stop. Listen. Collaborate.

One of the most important things I've learned is that you can't solve a problem until you understand what the problem actually is.

When someone calls upset, they're not necessarily angry at you. Maybe it's a billing problem, a technical issue, or a product that isn't doing what they expected. My first step is to listen and find out what they're trying to accomplish.

During my time at GoDaddy, I worked in both sales and technical support. I needed to understand the customer's project and what they wanted to do before suggesting a product. I could have recommended the biggest package, but that wasn't always what they needed. Sometimes they just wanted email. Other times they really did need something more advanced.

When someone had purchased the wrong product, I could refund it and help them get the right one, whether it cost more or less. Taking time to understand their needs helped avoid repeat calls, unnecessary refunds, and more frustration later.

Stop. Listen. Collaborate. There might be a song in there somewhere.

It's about people, not just tickets.

Good customer service isn't just making a sale or closing a ticket. It's finding the right solution, explaining why it makes sense, and making sure the person understands what happens next.

I've done that over the phone, in homes and businesses, and in schools. The technology changes, but the approach doesn't. Listen first, then work with the person to get it right.

The work behind the words

Technical support, consultative sales, billing and product questions, refunds and product changes, patient troubleshooting, and clear explanations.
