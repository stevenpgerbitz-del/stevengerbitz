01 / Fiber Optics / The story

Getting the connection right.

What I enjoyed most wasn't just the work with fiber. It was the people I met and what getting connected meant to them.

A little background

More than another installation.

One of the things I enjoyed most about working in fiber optics was traveling across rural America and meeting the people in those communities.

Many of the places we visited had very limited internet options. Some families were still using dial-up, others relied on satellite, and some had no internet access at all. Seeing their excitement when we connected them to fiber for the first time was one of the most rewarding parts of the job.

I remember one kid who had gotten an Xbox for Christmas but had never been able to use it online because all they had was dial-up. When we finished the installation and he connected it to the internet, his eyes got big. He was finally discovering something other kids already took for granted.

Sometimes installing internet really did make someone's life a little better.

Doing work I'm proud to leave behind.

Every home and business is different. I always started by asking the customer where they wanted the equipment. Then I'd figure out the best way to get the cable there. Sometimes that meant going through an exterior wall, even though it wasn't my favorite approach. Other times it meant working through attics, crawl spaces, or fishing cable through walls.

If a customer's preferred location would cause unnecessary damage or make the installation overly complicated, I'd explain why and give them other options. I never minded doing extra work if it meant a cleaner installation that performed well and made the customer happy. After all, they're the ones who have to look at it every day, not me.

I've never subscribed to the “looks good from my house” way of doing things.

When I finished, I'd test the connection, help customers set up their devices, walk them through the equipment, and show them what I'd done. If I accidentally caused damage, I told them. I wanted customers to understand the work and feel comfortable with the result.

Sharing what I learned.

At Vantage Point, I also enjoyed helping newer technicians learn the trade on actual jobs. I trained approximately 15 technicians one-on-one. I'd show them the company's methods and expectations, have them watch and ask questions, and then let them do the work while I observed.

Once they could do the job correctly, I was happy for them to develop their own approach, as long as it met company standards. I believed in checking the physical installation and the connection, not just assuming the job was done right.

The work behind the words

Residential and business installations, fusion splicing, OTDR and optical testing, cable routing, customer setup, documentation, and one-on-one field training.
